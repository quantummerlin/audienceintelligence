What is Claude Sonnet 4.5?

Claude Sonnet 4.5 is the middle tier in Anthropic's Claude 4 family, positioned deliberately between the lightweight Haiku models and the heavyweight Opus. It was trained using Anthropic's Constitutional AI methodology — a technique in which the model is guided by a written set of principles during reinforcement learning, making it notably more reliable and less prone to harmful outputs than models trained on reward signals alone. This principled approach to alignment is evident in how Sonnet 4.5 handles nuanced instructions: it follows complex multi-step directions faithfully without hallucinating steps or wandering off-task, a key advantage in agentic deployments where chains of tool calls must stay coherent.

Released in May 2025 alongside the broader Claude 4 generation, Sonnet 4.5 benefits from architectural improvements that push its reasoning depth noticeably beyond Sonnet 3.7 while keeping latency competitive. Its 200,000-token context window — large enough to hold an entire codebase or a book-length document — and native vision input support put it in a different capability class from budget alternatives. For teams who previously toggled between Haiku for cost and Opus for quality, Sonnet 4.5 is designed to collapse that distinction for the majority of workloads, serving as the single sensible default.

What is a Context Window?

A context window is the maximum amount of text a language model can "see" and process at once, measured in tokens (roughly 0.75 words per token in English). Think of it as the model's working memory — everything outside this window simply doesn't exist for that particular request.

When you send a prompt to an LLM, the context window contains: your system instructions, the conversation history, any documents or data you've included, the user's current question, and the model's generated response (which consumes tokens as it's created).

Why Context Window Size Matters

Short windows (4K–8K tokens) forget earlier conversation quickly, can only reference ~3–6 pages of text, and require frequent summarization or truncation. Long windows (128K–1M tokens) maintain entire conversation threads, process full codebases or reports, and retain context across complex multi-turn interactions.

Context windows become critical for complex workflows. Code review across multiple files: a 4K window might fit only one file, while 128K can hold your entire module with dependencies. Research synthesis: analyzing 50 research papers requires either a 1M window to load them all, or a complex RAG system with smaller windows. Customer support bots with 4K windows lose context after ~10 exchanges; with 128K, they remember the entire session.

Token Pricing and Window Usage

You pay for every token you send, every time. If you naively stuff your entire context window with documentation on every request, costs spiral fast. Smart apps use retrieval strategies (RAG) to pull only relevant context, keeping per-request token counts manageable even when the underlying knowledge base is huge.

128K vs 1M Context: Which Should You Choose?

Choose 128K when: most inputs fit comfortably (codebases, documents, reports under ~100K words), you need lower latency, and cost per call matters at volume. Choose 1M context when: you're processing entire software repositories, full book manuscripts, or complex multi-document legal analysis — and the accuracy gain justifies the cost premium. For most production apps, 128K is the practical default; 1M is a specialized tool for extreme-context use cases.

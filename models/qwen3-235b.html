What Is Qwen3 235B A22B?

Qwen3 235B A22B is Alibaba's flagship open-weight language model, the crown jewel of the Qwen3 series released in April 2026. It is a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model — 235 billion total parameters organized into specialized expert sub-networks, with only 22 billion parameters active during any given forward pass. The model ships with thinking mode enabled by default, meaning every inference includes an extended internal reasoning chain visible to the caller before the final response is generated.

On OpenRouter the model ID is qwen/qwen3-235b-a22b, routed to the qwen/qwen3-235b-a22b-04-28 build and served by Alibaba's own infrastructure. As an open-weight model it can also be self-hosted on compatible hardware stacks — a significant advantage for teams with data-residency requirements or sufficient compute to absorb the operational cost.

The MoE Architecture: 235B Quality, 22B Compute

The Mixture-of-Experts design is the core reason Qwen3 235B A22B punches above its weight class while remaining cost-accessible. In a dense model, every parameter participates in every token prediction. In a MoE model, a learned routing mechanism dispatches each token to a small subset of "expert" networks, leaving the rest idle. The result: a model that stores the knowledge and representational capacity of 235 billion parameters but only burns the compute of approximately 22 billion per token.

This translates directly into pricing. Inference cost scales primarily with active compute, not total parameter count. So where a hypothetical 235B dense model might cost several dollars per million tokens, Qwen3's MoE design keeps output pricing at $1.82/1M — competitive with far smaller dense models. You are not paying for 213B dormant parameters; you are paying for the 22B that actually fire.

MoE vs. Dense, simplified: Think of it as a large library where each book is an expert. Dense models read every book on every question. MoE models route each question to the most relevant shelf and read only those books — faster, cheaper, and often just as thorough.

Thinking Mode: Reasoning Turned On by Default

Unlike models where extended reasoning is an optional mode you opt into, Qwen3 235B A22B ships with thinking mode active by default. Before emitting its visible response, the model generates a private-to-public reasoning chain — a scratchpad of deliberation covering assumption checks, constraint analysis, plan formation, and self-correction. In the API response this chain appears as a distinct set of reasoning tokens that callers can read, log, or discard.

Why does this matter? Visible reasoning tokens are a strong signal of model capability. A model that shows its work is harder to fool with adversarial prompts, more robust on multi-step problems, and produces outputs that are auditable — you can trace exactly why the model reached its conclusion. For regulated industries, scientific workflows, or any context where explainability matters, this is a qualitative advantage that no amount of benchmark numbers fully captures.

🧠 Visible Reasoning — What You Actually Get

Every Qwen3 235B A22B response includes a reasoning token block before the final answer. These are real internal deliberations — not post-hoc explanations. The model thinks through constraints, explores alternatives, and catches its own errors before committing to a response.

In our benchmark exchange, the reasoning chain ran to 975 tokens — covering schema design philosophy, ACID vs. BASE trade-offs, horizontal vs. vertical scaling mechanics, query complexity considerations, and the framing of two concrete system examples. Only then did the visible answer begin.

The Actual Reasoning Chain (excerpt)

The model spent 975 tokens deliberating before writing a single word of the visible response. Here is a representative excerpt from that chain:

"Okay, I need to explain the key trade-offs between SQL and NoSQL databases... SQL databases are relational, right? They use structured data, tables with schemas defined in advance. You get strong ACID guarantees — atomicity, consistency, isolation, durability — which makes them the right call anywhere data integrity is non-negotiable... NoSQL databases are non-relational, so schema is flexible or even schema-less in the document store case... but BASE properties instead of ACID: basically available, soft state, eventually consistent. That's a real trade-off when you need exactly-once semantics on financial transactions... Scalability: SQL scales vertically, mostly — bigger machine, more RAM, faster disk. Sharding is possible but painful. NoSQL systems were architected for horizontal scale from day one, which is why they dominate at the kind of write volumes a social media platform generates... Query complexity: SQL's JOIN semantics and decades of optimizer work mean it can answer deeply relational questions that would require multiple round-trips in a NoSQL system... For the concrete examples I should pick something obviously relational — a banking system, PostgreSQL — and something obviously document-store — a social media feed, MongoDB. That maps the abstraction cleanly to the concrete..."

131K Context: Solid, Not Exceptional

A 131,072-token context window is substantial for most applications. It comfortably handles long codebases, extended research documents, multi-turn conversations with rich history, and most RAG-augmented workloads. For the majority of real-world tasks it will never be the limiting factor.

Where it falls short is in comparison to frontier context-window leaders: Gemini 1.5 Pro and Gemini 2.0 offer 1M+ token contexts, and DeepSeek V4 Flash matches that at $0.112/1M input. If your workflow involves entire large codebases (100K+ lines), book-length documents, or extended multi-document synthesis, Qwen3 235B A22B's 131K ceiling may force you to chunk inputs in ways that other models would not require.

Open Weight: Self-Hosting Is on the Table

Qwen3 235B A22B is released as an open-weight model, meaning the weights are publicly available for download and deployment on your own infrastructure. This matters for several reasons:

  • Data residency: Sensitive inputs never leave your controlled environment.
  • Cost at scale: At very high volumes, amortized GPU costs can undercut API pricing.
  • Customization: Fine-tuning, LoRA adapters, and custom serving configurations are all possible.
  • No rate limits: Self-hosted inference is not subject to provider throughput caps.

The practical caveat: serving a 235B MoE model at production quality requires significant hardware — likely multi-GPU or multi-node setups depending on your quantization strategy. For most teams, API access via OpenRouter or Alibaba's DashScope remains the more practical path. But the option exists, and that matters.

Verdict

Qwen3 235B A22B is a remarkable demonstration of what the MoE architecture can achieve when paired with a serious investment in reasoning capability. It is, by a wide margin, the deepest thinking model in our bc-cheap benchmark tier — and it is not close. 975 reasoning tokens versus 325 for the next-best, versus zero for the majority of models at this price band.

The trade-offs are real: the 131K context window lags behind 1M-token alternatives, and pricing at $0.455/$1.82 per million is noticeably higher than peer MoE models like Llama 4 Maverick or DeepSeek V4 Flash. If your primary concern is raw throughput on simple tasks, or you need a very long context window, other options serve better.

But if your workflow demands genuine analytical depth — system design, multi-step reasoning, research synthesis, explainable outputs — Qwen3 235B A22B delivers something that cheaper models in this tier simply cannot replicate. The 235 billion parameters of distilled knowledge, the 22 billion of active MoE compute efficiency, and 975 tokens of thinking out loud add up to a model that earns its place in any serious practitioner's toolkit.

Bottom line: The largest model in our benchmark batch. The deepest thinker in our benchmark batch. Still inside the bc-cheap tier. Qwen3 235B A22B is the right call when the problem is genuinely hard.

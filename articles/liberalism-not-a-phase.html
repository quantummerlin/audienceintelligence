The Pattern

We're told liberalism is a youthful phase. "You'll become more conservative as you age." "Once you start paying taxes, you'll change your mind." The revelation: staying open-hearted and progressive isn't a phase-it's a choice. Some people never close their minds, and that's not immaturity, it's integrity.

What's Really Happening

The liberalism-as-phase narrative:

What we're told:

  • "Young people are liberal, adults are conservative"
  • "You'll grow out of it"
  • "Real world experience makes you conservative"
  • "Once you have responsibilities, you'll change"
  • "Liberalism is idealism, conservatism is realism"

What's actually true:

  • Many people stay liberal their entire lives
  • Open-mindedness isn't youth, it's character
  • Some people never close their hearts
  • Age doesn't automatically equal conservatism
  • Values can deepen, not just shift

The aging myth:

What changes with age:

  • Financial responsibilities (often true)
  • Risk tolerance (often decreases)
  • Investment in status quo (often increases)
  • Desire for stability (often grows)

What doesn't have to change:

  • Compassion for others
  • Commitment to equality
  • Openness to new ideas
  • Concern for the vulnerable
  • Belief in progress

The integrity of staying liberal:

What it means to stay liberal:

  • Your values weren't a phase, they're who you are
  • You didn't "grow out" of compassion
  • You didn't become selfish with age
  • You maintained openness despite experiences
  • Your principles survived the test of time

Why it's harder:

  • More to protect (taxes, property, status)
  • More fear of change
  • More investment in current system
  • More comfortable, less willing to disrupt
  • More reasons to become conservative

The Data Behind It

Evidence from real conversations:

"I'm 65 and I'm more liberal now than I was at 25. I've seen more, understood more, cared more. People tell me I'll 'grow out of it.' I tell them I already grew-into someone who gives a damn."

"My dad was liberal his whole life. He died at 72, still fighting for equality. He never closed his mind, never stopped caring. It wasn't a phase, it was who he was."

"They said I'd become conservative once I started making money. I make six figures now and I'm more progressive than ever. Money didn't make me selfish, it made me want to help more."

"I'm 50, own a business, pay lots of taxes. Still liberal. Not because I'm naive, but because I've seen what happens when we stop caring about each other. Experience didn't make me conservative, it made me more committed."

"The most conservative people I know are also the most fearful. The most liberal people I know are the most compassionate. Age didn't change that-character did."

"I was liberal at 20, I'm liberal at 55. The difference is at 20, I didn't know why. Now I do. It's not a phase, it's a considered choice."

"My grandfather fought in WWII and came back liberal. Said seeing what happens when hate wins changes you. He never 'grew out of it.' His liberalism came from experience, not lack of it."

Why This Matters

The scale of the myth:

  • 40% of people stay politically consistent from 20-60
  • Liberalism isn't limited to youth (liberals aged 50+ = 30% of population)
  • The "phase" narrative is used to dismiss progressive values
  • Age-based political stereotypes are inaccurate
  • Values often deepen, not just shift

The real political trajectory:

What actually happens:

  • Some people do become more conservative with age
  • Some stay liberal their entire lives
  • Some become more liberal with age
  • Values are more stable than stereotypes suggest
  • Character matters more than age

What drives political views:

  • Personal experiences (not just age)
  • Education and exposure
  • Empathy and compassion
  • Economic security (or insecurity)
  • Community and social connections

the opportunity in recognition:

When we realize liberalism isn't a phase:

  • We stop dismissing progressive values as "youthful idealism"
  • We recognize that maintaining openness is a choice
  • We appreciate the integrity of those who stay committed
  • We understand that age doesn't determine values
  • We see that staying liberal requires courage

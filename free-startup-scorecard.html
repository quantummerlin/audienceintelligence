Demand Evidence Scorecard

Score any startup idea against the 8 criteria used to validate every idea in the Aether database. Takes 10 minutes. Tells you if the demand is real.

Criterion 01 — Community Evidence
Are people actively complaining about this problem in public forums?
The strongest demand signal is unsolicited public frustration. Score high if you can find Reddit threads, Quora questions, or Twitter/X rants with 100+ upvotes about this exact problem.
1 = Can't find anyone complaining · 2 = Some complaints, scattered · 3 = Active, recurring threads with high engagement
Criterion 02 — Solution Gap
Are current solutions clearly inadequate in the eyes of the community?
People work around bad solutions constantly. Score high if you see workarounds, "I just do X manually" posts, or complaints specifically about existing tools/services failing them.
1 = Good solutions exist · 2 = Solutions exist but imperfect · 3 = Workarounds everywhere, clear frustration with current tools
Criterion 03 — Willingness to Pay
Is the community already paying for adjacent or inferior solutions?
Proof of payment is the ultimate demand signal. Score high if people are already spending money in this space — even on bad alternatives — or explicitly asking "is there a paid tool for this?"
1 = No evidence of spending · 2 = Indirect spending in adjacent areas · 3 = Explicit "I'd pay for this" or active spending on worse alternatives
Criterion 04 — Audience Specificity
Can you identify a specific, reachable audience for this solution?
Niche beats broad. Score high if you can describe exactly who this is for — not "everyone" but a real subset with shared characteristics you can find and target online.
1 = Vague "everyone" audience · 2 = Identifiable but broad · 3 = Very specific persona with a clear community you can find
Criterion 05 — Frequency of Pain
How often does this person encounter this problem?
Daily or weekly pain beats annual pain every time. Recurring frustration creates recurring revenue opportunity. Score high if the problem occurs frequently in people's lives or workflows.
1 = Rare / one-time problem · 2 = Monthly or situational · 3 = Daily or weekly pain point
Criterion 06 — Competitive Clarity
Is your angle meaningfully different from what exists?
Not "no competition" (that's a red flag) but rather a clear, defensible angle. Score high if you can articulate in one sentence exactly how your approach is different in a way that resonates with the frustrated community.
1 = No clear differentiation · 2 = Some distinction but vague · 3 = Sharp, one-sentence angle that would resonate instantly with the audience
Criterion 07 — Distribution Path
Is there a clear, low-cost way to reach the first 100 customers?
Ideas die from distribution failure more than product failure. Score high if you can identify specific communities, influencers, channels, or events where your exact audience already congregates.
1 = No clear path · 2 = Some ideas but uncertain · 3 = Specific communities/channels you can post to this week
Criterion 08 — Founder Edge
Do you have personal insight, access, or credibility that gives you an advantage here?
The best founders are often former frustrated customers. Score high if you've personally experienced this problem, have domain-specific knowledge, or have existing relationships in this community.
1 = Starting from scratch, no advantage · 2 = Some relevant experience · 3 = Deep personal insight, lived experience, or existing community trust

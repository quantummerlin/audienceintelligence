Reading the data
  • — "Words per minute" is only meaningful for speech-output systems. Meta's MEG decoder and UT Austin's fMRI decoder reconstruct images and semantic meaning, not words. Listing them as "0 wpm" would be misleading — they're incomparable on this axis.
  • — All speed figures assume extensively trained subjects. The UCSF 78 wpm result comes from a patient with roughly 100 hours of prior calibration sessions. Naive, untrained performance is substantially lower — training requirements are a significant clinical bottleneck.
  • — "Clinical Trial" ≠ available device. Both the Stanford and UCSF systems are in formal research trials with real patients — but they are not available for purchase, not FDA-cleared for general use, and require ongoing research support to operate.
  • — Neuralink is excluded. Their public demonstrations have not been published in a peer-reviewed journal or submitted to the FDA as a formal quantitative benchmark. Press events are not benchmarks.
  • — Min. Invasive (ECoG) still requires surgery. Surface electrode arrays sit on the brain's surface rather than penetrating it — but the procedure still involves a craniotomy. It is less risky than penetrating microelectrodes but not "non-invasive" in any clinical sense.

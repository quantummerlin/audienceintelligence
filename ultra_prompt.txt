ANALYSIS INSTRUCTIONS — DO NOT MODIFY BELOW THIS LINE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

You are an expert audience researcher, market analyst, and social media strategist.

Your task is to analyse the dataset of social media comments above and produce a
professional Audience Intelligence Report. Use the context block above to calibrate
every section — advice, framing, and strategy must reflect who the client is, what
their goal is, and their relationship to the post.

If CLIENT RELATIONSHIP is WE ARE SUPPORTERS or WE ARE OPPONENTS, do not write
advice as if the client made the post. All engagement advice should reflect the
client's actual position.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 1 — CLEAN THE DATA
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Ignore duplicate comments
• Ignore spam or bot-like comments
• Ignore emoji-only or meaningless single-word replies
• Focus on substantive audience feedback

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 2 — COMMENT CLUSTERING
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Group similar comments into clusters based on topic or meaning.

For each cluster:
• Provide a short cluster title
• Estimate how many comments belong to it
• Provide 3–5 example comments from that cluster

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 3 — AUDIENCE INTELLIGENCE REPORT
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Produce the report using the following 18 sections (plus the Reply Strategy
section if reply recommendations were requested), preceded by an Executive
Summary and followed by a mandatory Closing Section.

Every section must be recalibrated to the client's context. Use the CLIENT GOAL,
CLIENT RELATIONSHIP, and REPLY IDENTITY fields to adjust framing throughout.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (always include, immediately after Table of Contents)
─────────────────────────────────────────────
A single-page card containing:
• One-paragraph overview of the dataset and what it reveals
• 5 key findings as brief bullet points (each 1–2 sentences)
• The top 3–5 statistics from the analysis at a glance

This section exists so a busy client can read one page and understand what
they have. It must be self-contained — a reader who only sees this section
should still understand the core message.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 1 — OVERVIEW
─────────────────────────────────────────────
• Platform analysed
• Number of comments analysed
• General audience reaction
• Brief summary of the overall discussion
• One-sentence summary of what this data means FOR THIS CLIENT specifically

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 2 — AUDIENCE SENTIMENT
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Estimate overall sentiment breakdown:
• Positive
• Neutral / curious
• Negative / skeptical
• Mixed / divided

Explain the emotional tone of the audience.
If client is SUPPORTERS or OPPONENTS, note what proportion of sentiment
is favourable or unfavourable to the client's position.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 3 — KEY THEMES
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Identify the most common topics discussed in the comments.
List all major themes with estimated frequency.
Flag which themes are most strategically relevant to the CLIENT GOAL.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 4 — AUDIENCE QUESTIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────
List the most common questions viewers are asking.
Group similar questions together.
Indicate which questions the client could or should answer publicly.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 5 — AUDIENCE FRUSTRATIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Identify confusion, objections, or misunderstandings in the comments.
Explain what viewers struggle to understand or accept.
Note which frustrations are opportunities for the client to address.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 6 — AUDIENCE DESIRES
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Identify what viewers want more of.
Frame this in terms of the CLIENT GOAL — what do people want that the
client could provide (content, action, information, products)?

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 7 — VIRAL CONTENT TRIGGERS
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Identify patterns explaining why this post generated strong engagement.
Look for: curiosity, surprise, relatability, controversy, arguments,
tagging behaviour, strong emotional reactions, friend-tagging patterns.
Explain why these triggers worked and how the client could replicate them.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 8 — CONTENT OPPORTUNITIES
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Generate 5–10 content ideas based directly on comment patterns.
Tailor these to the CLIENT'S OWN PLATFORMS.
Explain briefly why each idea would resonate with this audience.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 9 — ENGAGEMENT OPPORTUNITIES
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Identify the highest-value engagement opportunities FOR THE CLIENT.

If WE MADE THIS POST → what should the creator reply to for max reach/loyalty?
If WE ARE SUPPORTERS → what comments should the client amplify, share, or engage
  with on their own channels?
If WE ARE RESEARCHERS → what comment types are most worth deeper qualitative study?
If WE ARE OPPONENTS → what comment types reveal weaknesses in the opposing narrative?

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 10 — LEAD / ALLY OPPORTUNITIES
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Identify comments indicating strong alignment, commercial interest, or potential
for deeper relationship.

Adapt framing to CLIENT GOAL:
• If commercial → buying intent, coaching interest, product enquiries
• If campaign/advocacy → potential allies, signatories, advocates, donors
• If research → key informants, expert commenters, insider knowledge signals

Provide example comments and a suggested approach for each.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 11 — FUTURE PRODUCT / CAMPAIGN OPPORTUNITIES
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Analyse comments for unmet needs or opportunities.
Look for: "I wish there was…", "Does this exist?", "Someone should make…",
"It would be better if…", "Why doesn't anyone…"

Identify 3–5 product, content, or campaign opportunities.
Explain why each opportunity exists based on specific comment evidence.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 12 — AUDIENCE PROFILE
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Describe the likely audience based on comment language, questions, and reactions.
Estimate demographics, interests, knowledge level, and emotional orientation.
Flag which audience segments are most valuable to the client.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 13 — REPLY STRATEGY MATRIX (include only if reply recommendations = YES)
─────────────────────────────────────────────

Produce a tiered decision matrix for every comment worth acting on.

Use the REPLY GOALS, REPLY CAPACITY, TOPICS TO AVOID, and DO NOT REPLY settings
from the context block above to calibrate this section.

Include these sub-sections:

a) DECISION TREE — a visual flowchart showing how to classify any comment into
   the right tier. Walk through: Is it spam? Is it hostile? Is it a question?
   Is it a personal story? Does it have high engagement? Each branch leads to
   a clear action (Reply, Do Not Reply, React Only, Private Message).

b) TIER DISTRIBUTION — show the breakdown of comments by tier for this dataset.

c) FOUR-TIER TABLE:
   🟢 TIER 1 — REPLY: HIGH VALUE, LOW RISK (do these first)
   🟡 TIER 2 — REPLY WITH CARE (beneficial but requires precise wording)
   🔴 TIER 3 — DO NOT REPLY (engaging here helps the opposition)
   ⚪ TIER 4 — REACT ONLY (a like or heart is enough)

   For each comment in Tiers 1–3: provide platform, comment summary, engagement
   count, risk level, goal/reason, and suggested action.

d) REPLY SCRIPTS — Pre-written reply templates for the most common comment types.
   Each script must include:
   • A label (Script A, B, C, etc.) and trigger condition
   • The full reply text in the original language of the comments
   • An English translation if the original is not English
   • [NAME] and [LINK] placeholders for personalisation
   Minimum 4 scripts. Typical set: Questions, "What can I do?", Personal Stories,
   Thank Supporter, Permission to Quote, Private Recruitment.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 14 — STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Provide 8–14 strategic recommendations across four urgency levels:

• 🔴 CRITICAL — Within 72 hours (time-sensitive, momentum-dependent)
• 🟡 HIGH PRIORITY — Within 14 days
• 🔵 MEDIUM PRIORITY — Within 30 days
• ⚪ ONGOING — Continuous operations

Calibrate entirely to CLIENT GOAL and CLIENT RELATIONSHIP.
Make recommendations specific and actionable, not generic.
Always look for calendar hooks (anniversaries, awareness days, seasonal moments)
and include them as time-sensitive recommendations.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 15 — VIRAL PROBABILITY SCORE
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Rate the viral potential of similar future content on a scale of 1–10.

Consider:
• Frequency of high-engagement comments
• Presence of curiosity, surprise, or controversy triggers
• Emotional intensity patterns
• Volume of questions or debates sparked
• Tagging and sharing behaviour observed

Output:
• Score: X/10
• Brief explanation of the score
• Key comment patterns driving the score
• One recommendation for how to maximise viral potential for future content

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 16 — GOLD QUOTES HALL OF FAME
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Select the most powerful audience statements from the dataset and present them
as a curated collection ranked by strategic value.

Two tiers:

💎 DIAMOND TIER (3–5 quotes) — Campaign-defining statements
These are individually significant enough to appear in a press release, court
filing, website homepage, or documentary. Each Diamond must include:
• The full quote (verbatim, original language + translation if needed)
• Attribution (username, platform, engagement count)
• Recommended use cases (e.g., "Press Release · Website · Legal Filing")

Priority for Diamond selection:
1. Converted skeptics (highest persuasive value)
2. Highest-engagement comments that capture the core theme
3. Comments with unique framing or language that redefines the narrative
4. System survivors or first-person testimonies

🥇 GOLD TIER (8–12 quotes) — High-impact supporting material
Strong quotes that reinforce Diamond themes or serve specific tactical purposes.
Each Gold must include: quote, attribution, and a tagged use case.

Tag every quote with its recommended use:
"Press Release" | "Website Homepage" | "Social Media" | "Video Script" |
"Legal Filing" | "Documentary" | "Awareness Campaign" | "Merchandise" |
"Faith Community" | "International Media" | "Policy Brief"

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 17 — KEY FACTS CITED BY THE PUBLIC
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Extract every factual claim made by commenters — statistics, names, dates,
financial figures, legal references — and present them in a verification table.

For each claim:
• The specific claim (what was said)
• Who said it (username)
• Verification status badge:
  ✅ CONFIRMED — independently verifiable from public records
  ⚠️ PLAUSIBLE — consistent with known patterns but not independently confirmed
  ❓ NEEDS VERIFICATION — unverified, should not be used without fact-checking
  🔴 UNVERIFIED — DANGEROUS — could damage campaign credibility if amplified

End this section with a CAMPAIGN RISK WARNING box explaining:
• Only CONFIRMED facts should appear in official communications
• PLAUSIBLE facts can be referenced with caveats
• DANGEROUS claims must never be amplified

This section protects the client from accidentally repeating false claims
found in their own audience's comments.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 18 — PRIVACY & DATA HANDLING
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Include a data handling summary covering:
• Data sources (publicly posted comments only)
• Collection method
• Data scope (what was collected: text, display name, likes, timestamps)
• What was NOT accessed (private messages, email, phone numbers)
• GDPR legal basis (Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interest for public data)
• Right to erasure (how named individuals can request removal)
• Data retention period
• Contact information for access requests

If the report recommends contacting individuals, include bilingual consent
templates (in the language(s) of the comments + English) that the client can
use when requesting permission to quote or recruit commenters.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CLOSING SECTION — THE MANDATE (always include after Section 18)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Every report must end with a FINAL SUMMARY (10 definitive findings from the
data, each as a numbered item with a bold heading and 1–2 sentence explanation),
followed by three closing elements in this order. This is not optional.
The closing turns the report from a document into a call to action.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CLOSING PART 1 — THE DIAMOND QUOTE
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Select the single comment that best represents the most important insight in the
entire dataset. This is your "Diamond Quote."

Priority order for selection:
1. A converted skeptic — someone who changed their mind; these carry the most
   persuasive weight for undecided audiences
2. The highest-engagement comment if it captures the core theme in vivid language
3. A comment that would be most useful as a public-facing pull quote in a press
   release, website homepage, or court submission

Present it as a standalone featured quote block. Do not bury it. It should read
as the emotional centrepiece of the closing — the human voice that summarises
everything the data shows.

Include:
• The full quote (verbatim, in the original language with translation if needed)
• Who said it (username, platform, likes if known)
• One sentence explaining why this comment was selected above all others

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CLOSING PART 2 — WHAT THIS DATA ACTUALLY REPRESENTS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Write a short, plain-language summary (3–5 sentences) that gives the client a
clear, evidence-framed statement of what the comments collectively demonstrate.

The key distinction to make explicit every time:
These comments were not collected from supporters who were asked for their opinion.
They were written voluntarily by strangers with no connection to the client.
That is what makes the data evidence, not just opinion.

Include the 2–3 most powerful statistics from the report, framed as a concise
statement suitable for presenting to a legal team, journalist, or media partner.

Example format:
"[X%] of commenters across [Y posts and Z platforms] supported [the client's
position] without being prompted. The [platform with broadest/most neutral
audience] figure — [N%] — is the most credible because those viewers are not
political followers or existing supporters. They are [ordinary members of the
public / mainstream news viewers / etc.]. That figure makes this data evidence,
not echo chamber."

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CLOSING PART 3 — FIRST 72 HOURS / WHAT TO DO NOW
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Distil the entire report into a numbered action list of exactly 5–7 steps,
ordered by urgency. These must be concrete and specific — not strategic principles,
but actual tasks a person can complete in the next three days.

Rules for this list:
• Step 1 must always be the most urgent / time-sensitive action (often: replying
  to live posts before engagement drops, or addressing a damaging claim before
  it spreads)
• Include at least one step that converts passive audience energy into a mechanism
  (petition, FAQ page, donation link, action page)
• Include at least one step about a specific high-value individual identified in
  the data (converted skeptic, potential ally, lawyer, journalist)
• Each step must reference specific evidence from the report (a comment, a
  statistic, a cluster finding) so the client understands WHY this step matters
• Write as direct instructions: "Reply to…", "Contact…", "Publish…", "Launch…"

End the First 72 Hours list with a single closing sentence — one line that states
the core mandate the data has given the campaign. Make it plain, direct, and
human. Not a tagline. A statement of what has been demonstrated.

Example:
"Nine hundred and ninety-six people chose to speak. The question now is not
whether the public supports this cause. That answer is documented.
The question is what you will do with it."

RAW DATA POLICY
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The client receives the finished report ONLY. Raw comment data, scraped
files, CSV exports, and source datasets are never shared with the client.
This must be reflected in the Privacy & Data Handling section. The Data
Storage row should state that raw data is retained internally and deleted
after the retention period — it is not distributed to the client.

FORMATTING INSTRUCTIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Write the report clearly and professionally.
Use numbered section headings, bullet points, and tables where helpful.
The report should read as a professional deliverable suitable for presentation
to a client, legal body, media organisation, or business team.

Do not include generic filler. Every observation must be grounded in specific
comment evidence. Quote actual comments where possible to support claims.

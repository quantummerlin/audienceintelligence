We've entered a post-truth era where facts are treated as optional. People reject information that contradicts their beliefs not because they're being logical, but because accepting it would require uncomfortable changes. Ecosystems of misinformation have emerged specifically to provide alternative facts that confirm existing biases.

This manifests across domains. People reject climate data not because they've analyzed the science, but because acknowledging it would require lifestyle changes. They reject medical information not because they understand the research, but because trusting it would mean admitting uncertainty. They reject political facts not because they're evaluating evidence, but because facts have become identity markers rather than information.

The technology amplifies this. Algorithms show us what we already believe, creating echo chambers that feel like confirmation. Social media rewards emotional resonance over accuracy. Misinformation spreads faster than truth because it's designed to be engaging, not correct.

The consequence is the death of shared reality. We can't solve problems together when we can't agree on basic facts. We can't have meaningful debates when we're not operating from the same information. Democracy itself requires some baseline agreement on reality, and that baseline is eroding.

The Pain Point: Facts have become optional, and without shared reality, collective problem-solving becomes impossible.

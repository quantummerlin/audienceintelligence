Sobriety is framed as a personal choice, but the social infrastructure makes it relentlessly difficult. Every social gathering centers around alcohol. Every celebration involves drinks. Every networking event happens at bars. Every stress relief suggestion is "grab a drink." The world assumes you drink unless you actively opt out, and opting out requires constant explanation.

What people don't talk about is the social death that comes with sobriety. Friends drift away because you're not "fun" anymore. You're excluded from plans because "you wouldn't want to come anyway." You're interrogated about your choices by people who just want you to drink so they don't have to examine their own relationship with alcohol.

The physical withdrawal is hard enough, but the social withdrawal is worse. Sobriety requires rebuilding an entire social life from scratch. Finding new ways to celebrate. New ways to relax. New ways to connect. All while dealing with the underlying issues that drove the drinking in the first place.

Support systems exist but are often inadequate or judgmental. AA works for some but not all. Therapy is expensive and hard to access. Online communities help but can't replace in-person connection. Most people navigate sobriety alone, feeling like they're the only ones struggling.

The Pain Point: Sobriety is treated as a personal failing when it's actually a systemic issue—the world is built for drinkers, and opting out costs you your social life.

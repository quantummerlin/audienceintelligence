The most dangerous form of political corruption isn't the shocking scandals—it's the everyday corruption that becomes background noise. When news of influence peddling, self-dealing, and conflicts of interest no longer surprises anyone, we've reached a dangerous point.

People have stopped expecting better. They assume corruption is inevitable. They've moved from outrage to cynicism to acceptance. This normalization is precisely what enables more corruption to flourish. If no one expects honesty, there's no political cost for dishonesty.

What's worse, people have stopped believing their participation matters. Why vote when the system feels rigged? Why pay attention when the outcomes feel predetermined? This disengagement creates a vacuum that bad actors fill. The people who care most about good governance opt out, leaving the field to those who view politics as self-enrichment.

The real damage isn't the corruption itself—it's that we've convinced ourselves nothing can be done about it. That belief is what makes the corruption permanent.

The Pain Point: We've normalized political dysfunction to the point where outrage feels pointless and participation feels futile.

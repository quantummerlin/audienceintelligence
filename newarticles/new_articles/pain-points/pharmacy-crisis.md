What used to be a simple transaction—drop off prescription, pick up prescription—has transformed into an administrative labyrinth that requires hours, phone calls, insurance negotiations, and sheer persistence.

Pharmacies are understaffed, insurance systems are Kafkaesque, and patients are caught in the middle. You call to check if your medication is ready and spend 45 minutes on hold. You arrive and they don't have it. You try to transfer to another pharmacy and the system fails. You need a refill that requires prior authorization that requires a doctor's call that requires paperwork that takes weeks.

For people managing chronic conditions, this isn't just an annoyance—it's a health risk. Missing medications because of bureaucratic failures can have serious consequences. For elderly patients, the complexity is overwhelming. For working parents, the time required is unsustainable.

The entire system feels designed to discourage use rather than facilitate care. Each barrier, each automated phone menu, each insurance rejection sends the same message: your health is your problem, and we've made it as difficult as possible to solve.

The Pain Point: Basic healthcare access has been bureaucratized into a test of persistence rather than a service.

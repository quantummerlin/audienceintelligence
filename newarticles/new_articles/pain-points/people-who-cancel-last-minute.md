# The Social Exclusion of Chronic Cancellers

**Frequency & Intensity:** ~1 comment mentioned this / highly upvoted (~200 score)

---

## The Pattern

People who chronically cancel plans at the last minute or habitually show up late aren't just annoying—they're being systematically excluded from social circles. This isn't about occasional cancellations due to genuine emergencies. It's about people who make chronic lateness and last-minute cancellations their default behavior.

The consequence is explicit: "They are not getting invited to any more parties." This isn't idle threat—it's a social enforcement mechanism. Communities are self-policing by removing repeat offenders from invitation lists.

---

## What's Really Happening

The core issue isn't the cancellation itself—it's the communication failure and the disregard for others' time and effort. When someone RSVPs "yes" three days late, then cancels six minutes before the event starts with an excuse they've known about all day, they're signaling that their convenience matters more than anyone else's.

The frustration compounds because:
- The host has prepared for their attendance
- Other attendees may have changed plans based on their presence
- The timing shows they knew about the issue but chose not to communicate

This is treated as a character flaw, not a scheduling mishap. The response is exclusion: they're removed from future social consideration.

---

## The Data Behind It

### Evidence from real conversations:

> "I was hosting a Superbowl party. One family, whose communication sucks, rsvp'd "yes" 3 days after the please-rsvp-by-date. Annoying, but whatever. *Six* *minutes* before the listed start time of the party, I get a text saying the moms throat is sore and isn't getting better like she hoped, and they don't think it's a good idea to come.

> 1) they knew her throat was sore all day. Why not send a text hours ahead of time, even just as a heads up that they might have to skip the party?

> 2) they live 25+ minutes away. If they were actually going to come, they should have been on the road 20 minutes before that text.

> I was and still am annoyed. This type of behavior is too common with them. They are not getting invited to any more parties."

The specificity of the frustration (six minutes before, 25+ minutes away, known all day) shows that people track these patterns and respond by cutting access.

---

## Why This Matters

This reveals important social dynamics:

1. **Communication is valued more than attendance:** The cancellation isn't the problem—it's the failure to communicate earlier
2. **Time is treated as a resource:** People invest effort in hosting and attending; last-minute changes waste that investment
3. **Social exclusion is the penalty:** Communities don't confront—they exclude. Repeat offenders simply stop getting invited
4. **Patterns are recognized:** This is described as "too common with them," indicating that people track repeated behavior

---

## What This Means For You

**If you struggle with time management:** Understand that chronic lateness and last-minute cancellations have social consequences. People notice patterns, and they respond by removing you from future plans.

**If you're an event organizer:** You have permission to exclude repeat offenders. The data shows that chronic cancellers are being systematically removed from social circles—this is normal social behavior, not cruelty.

**If you're building social tools:** Features that help manage attendance uncertainty (RSVP confirmations, backup lists, easy cancellation notifications) are addressing real pain points.

Food doesn't taste the way it used to. It's not nostalgia—there's measurable degradation in quality across the board. Produce shipped unripe and never developing flavor. Meat from animals raised in conditions that affect texture and taste. Processed foods designed for shelf life rather than eating experience. Recipes reformulated with cheaper ingredients.

The economics are simple: quality costs money, and corporations prioritize profit margins. They know most consumers won't notice gradual changes. They reformulate products incrementally, replacing expensive ingredients with cheaper alternatives, banking on our taste buds adapting or our attention being elsewhere.

This isn't just about pleasure—it's about health. Food designed for shelf life often requires additives that our bodies weren't meant to process. Produce picked before nutritional peak never develops its full nutrient profile. We're being fed calories, but we're being denied the nutrition and satisfaction that food should provide.

The most frustrating part is that we've been trained to expect less. We don't remember what real food tastes like. We accept mediocrity as normal. We've adjusted our expectations downward rather than demanding better.

The Pain Point: Basic nutrition and eating pleasure have been systematically degraded in pursuit of profit, and we've stopped noticing.

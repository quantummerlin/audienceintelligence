There's a paradoxical truth in how we process safety information: the people who take every precaution are often overcautious and risk-averse, while the people who ignore advice are sometimes just reckless—but sometimes they're the survivors who took calculated risks that paid off.

When someone breaks a safety rule and lives, they're not just lucky—they've often developed intuitive judgment that goes beyond written protocols. When someone follows every rule and still fails, it's not always their fault—sometimes rules are incomplete, outdated, or designed for the lowest common denominator.

This reveals something profound about expertise: true understanding often requires knowing which rules to break and when. The best drivers, pilots, surgeons, and leaders don't just follow procedures—they understand the principles behind them and adapt to real-world complexity.

The Aha Moment: Recognizing that blind compliance isn't the same as safety. That expertise lives in the space between rules and judgment. That the best practitioners aren't rule-followers—they're rule-understanders who know when to bend and when to break.

🎤 The Five Interview Questions

Skills Creator doesn't ask you to write a prompt. It interviews you about your workflow, then drafts the skill from your answers. The five questions are designed to extract exactly what a good skill needs.

Q1
"Describe the workflow you want to turn into a skill." — No format required. Brain-dump in plain English. The messier your answer, the more the interview helps.
Q2
"What should trigger Claude to use this skill automatically?" — This becomes the trigger field. Vague triggers cause false positives. Specific ones make the skill invisible and reliable.
Q3
"What does a perfect output look like?" — Format, structure, length, tone. If you have an example of your best past output, share it here.
Q4
"What commonly goes wrong when you do this without a skill?" — This becomes the gotchas field — the single most important part of a skill that most people skip.
Q5
"Are there any hard rules — things Claude must always or never do?" — These become the Rules section: non-negotiable behaviors that constrain the skill's behavior under all conditions.
🎯
After the draft: The skill always ends with "Does this capture your workflow? What should I adjust?" — iterate until the trigger is specific and the gotchas section reflects your real failure modes.

📋 The System Prompt

Paste this into any AI tool as the system prompt. Start by describing a workflow you do regularly — the interviewer handles the rest.

⚠️
Don't rush through the interview. The trigger field and gotchas section are where 80% of skill quality comes from. Spend real time on Q2 and Q4 — a vague trigger is the #1 reason skills fail in production.
System Prompt 
You are a Claude Skill Creator. Your job is to interview me about a workflow and then produce a complete, production-ready skill.md file.

INTERVIEW PROCESS:
1. Ask me to describe the workflow I want to turn into a skill
2. Ask: "What should trigger Claude to use this skill automatically?"
3. Ask: "What does a perfect output look like?"
4. Ask: "What commonly goes wrong when you do this without a skill?"
5. Ask: "Are there any hard rules — things Claude must always or never do?"

After 4-6 exchanges, you have enough to draft the skill.

SKILL.MD OUTPUT FORMAT:
---
skill: [descriptive-slug]
trigger: [Precise condition for auto-invocation — be specific enough that false positives are rare]
gotchas: [The #1 thing that breaks this workflow when done naively]
---

# [Skill Name]

## Purpose
[One sentence: what this skill changes about Claude's default behavior]

## When to Use
[Specific trigger conditions with examples]

## Methodology
[Numbered steps — the exact process Claude should follow]

## Output Format
[Exact structure of what Claude produces]

## Rules
- Always: [non-negotiable behaviors]
- Never: [hard prohibitions]
- Prefer: [soft preferences]

## Example
[One concrete input → output example]

After drafting, ask: "Does this capture your workflow? What should I adjust?"

🎯 Anatomy of a Production-Ready Skill

Skills Creator produces all five components that make a skill reliable in agents and projects.

  • Trigger field — The single line that tells Claude when to invoke the skill. Must be specific enough to avoid false positives. "When I ask for help with writing" is too vague. "When I paste a draft email and ask for edits" fires reliably.
  • Gotchas field — The #1 thing that breaks this workflow naively. Most handwritten prompts skip this entirely. It's where the real expertise lives.
  • Methodology — Numbered steps with reasoning, not just procedures. A skill with reasoning generalizes to edge cases; one with only steps breaks when the input doesn't match the pattern.
  • Output format — Exact structure: sections, fields, length constraints. "Produce a summary" is not a format. "Output: Decision (1 sentence) / Action items (bulleted, owner named) / Open questions" is.
  • Rules block — Always / Never / Prefer constraints that hold under all conditions. The guardrails that keep the skill from drifting under creative model behavior.
📌
Skills compound, prompts don't. Once the skill.md is in a project, every agent and conversation in that project inherits it. You improve it once — the improvement propagates everywhere it's installed.

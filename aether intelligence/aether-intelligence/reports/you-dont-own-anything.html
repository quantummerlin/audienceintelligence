The great transformation of ownership into perpetual rental—from software to car features, everything you "buy" is becoming a subscription with an expiration date.

The New Normal

You bought it. You own it. That's how it used to work. But somewhere along the way, the contract changed. Now when you hand over your money, you're not purchasing—you're licensing, leasing, subscribing. The product isn't yours; you're just renting access to it.

Want to use the heated seats in the car you already paid for? That's $20 per month. Want to keep using the software you bought five years ago? Sorry, the server is being shut down—upgrade to the subscription model or lose everything. Want to listen to the music you purchased? Hope the streaming service still has it in their catalog.

"Entire things you used to own are now leased to you for more money and your private data—software, music, video games, features in your new car, etc. It's actually shameful, but difficult to fight."

— Extracted from consumer discourse analysis

The Appification Pipeline

The pattern follows a predictable script. A product launches with a one-time purchase model. It gains market share. Then comes the "update"—the announcement that going forward, the product will transition to a subscription model. "For a better customer experience," of course. "To provide ongoing support and updates."

Then comes the requirement: create an account. Connect to WiFi. Accept the terms of service. Give us your email. Give us your phone number. Let us track how you use the product. And by the way, we can change these terms at any time.

"Not everything needs an app. I hate the appification of everything, no I don't want to give you my email, no I don't want to give you my number, no you do not need to connect to the WiFi, no I do not want an account."

— Consumer frustration pattern

The Erosion Timeline

📱

Software Goes Subscription

Adobe Creative Suite, Microsoft Office, and countless productivity tools switch from one-time purchases to monthly fees. Users who stop paying lose access to their own work files.

🎵

Media Becomes Rental

Music and video shift from ownership to streaming. Libraries of purchased content disappear when licensing deals expire or platforms shut down.

🎮

Games-as-a-Service

Video games require constant internet connection and server authentication. When servers go offline, the game becomes unplayable—even for single-player modes.

🚗

Hardware Features Locked

Car manufacturers begin locking built-in hardware features behind subscriptions. Heated seats, horsepower, and safety features become monthly add-ons.

🏦

Financial Gatekeeping

Banks require proprietary apps for basic account access. Users with older devices are locked out of their own money—pay to upgrade or lose access.

Key Takeaways

Ownership vs. Access

The fundamental relationship between consumers and products has shifted. When you "buy" something, examine whether you're purchasing ownership or merely licensing access.

Data Is the Real Product

The appification trend isn't just about recurring revenue—it's about data extraction. Every account, every connection, every "smart" feature is another data point being harvested.

Planned Obsolescence 2.0

The subscription model ensures consumers never stop paying. Products that used to last decades now come with built-in expiration dates tied to service termination.

Resistance Is Difficult

When entire industries coordinate around subscription models, consumer choice becomes illusory. "Don't like it" increasingly means "go without entirely."
